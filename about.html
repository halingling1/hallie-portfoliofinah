About Me

Hallie Tran is a junior Digital Media student at San José State University pursuing a Bachelor of Fine Arts in Digital Media Art. Her work focuses on visual storytelling through video production, editing, animation, writing, and marketing. Inspired by documentary media and digital content, she is interested in creating meaningful stories that connect with audiences. Through her studies, she continues to develop her skills across multiple creative disciplines and explore the ways media can inform, engage, and bring people together.

Artist statement

To me, video is the medium that first proved to me how much power a story can engage an audience. When I was in high school, I was a part of my school’s Digital Media Academy where we worked with the school news program called PGTV. I often worked as a producer and a writer. Throughout my experience in this academy, I started to understand how story is less about what is filmed and it more about the choices used to produce the project to ensure that audience feels something through your work. My documentary about Lunar New Year, which became a finalist in the #SEVACON competition, further showed me that I am most drawn to stories rooted in culture and community.

Ever since attending SJSU, video is usually at the center of my work, while I also learn how to tell a story in other mediums. Within my first year, Art 74 and Art 75 taught me the foundations of Photoshop, Illustrator, HTML/CSS, video editing, and sound. From what I have gained, I have used these tools to convey more persona messages.

A prime example of this is my piece: After the Dance. For about six years I was a part of lion dance and served at the club’s president, so stepping away from something I was so passionate about meant letting go a major part of my identity. Instead of erasing that part of my life, I wanted to showcase how moving on does not mean forgetting. By combining old lion dance footage with animation I drew in Procreate, the footage represents documented memories, while the animation represents my own reflection on it. The red and gold of the performances contrast with darker, softer tones to separate celebration from reflection. At the end, the animated version of myself walks away from the lion dance imagery, which I intended to show acceptance and growth instead of rejection.

Beyond video, I am interested in the other way digital media can support a story. I have modeled a character in Blender, built an interactive paint program with custom brushes in p5.js, and made a short vide inspired by Perfect Blue. I am also becoming interested in marketing, in which I have started to explore as a part of the marketing staff for NorCal VSA, where I make graphics and edit photos and videos to promote out events. This work has shown that marketing is another form of storytelling, in which a message must reach people quickly and leave an impression, and it keeps me interested in strengthening my design skills.

Ultimately, my goal in the BFA program is to grow as a video maker and designer. I want to improve my directing, editing, animation, and sound, and to combine them with design so that mu work looks and feels intentional. In addition, I want to keep learning from my faculty and classmates who see media differently than I do. Above all, I want to create work that tells meaningful stories and reaches people who need to see them.
